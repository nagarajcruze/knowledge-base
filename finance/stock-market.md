## 1. Stock Market Indices

An index is a statistical measure of the performance of a segment of the stock market. It acts as a barometer for the overall economy or a specific sector.

### A. Popular Indian Indices
- **Nifty 50**: Represents the weighted average of the 50 largest and most liquid Indian companies listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE).
- **Sensex**: Represents the performance of the 30 largest, most actively traded companies on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE).
- **Nifty Next 50**: Represents the next 50 companies after the Nifty 50 (ranked 51–100 by size). These are potential future Nifty 50 entrants.

### B. How Indices are Constructed
1. **Free-Float Market Capitalization**: Most modern indices use this method, where weightings are based only on the value of shares available for public trading (excluding promoter holdings, government stakes, etc.).
2. **Rebalancing**: Index committees periodically review and reconstitute the index (usually semi-annually) to add rising companies and remove underperforming or shrinking companies, ensuring the index stays healthy.

## 2. Fundamental Analysis vs. Technical Analysis

Investors use two primary methodologies to analyze stocks and make buying or selling decisions:

| Feature | Fundamental Analysis (FA) | Technical Analysis (TA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Concept** | Evaluate the intrinsic value of a company. | Forecast future price movements based on past price and volume data. |
| **Data Sources** | Financial statements (Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss, Cash Flow), industry trends, management quality, economic indicators. | Stock charts, historical price patterns, volume indicators (e.g., RSI, MACD, Moving Averages). |
| **Time Horizon** | Long-term investing (months, years, or decades). | Short-term trading (minutes, hours, days, or weeks). |
| **Goal** | Buy undervalued stocks and hold them until they reach their true value. | Identify entry and exit points to capture short-term price trends. |

## 3. Advanced Ratios

### A. Valuation Ratios
- **P/E (Price-to-Earnings) Ratio**: The ratio of the current share price to the company’s earnings per share (EPS).
  - *Interpretation*: Generally, overpriced/valued stocks have a high P/E, while undervalued/cheaper stocks have a low P/E.
- **P/B (Price-to-Book) Ratio**: The ratio of the current share price to the book value per share.
  - *Interpretation*: A lower P/B could indicate that the stock is undervalued, or it could mean there are fundamental problems with the company's business model.

### B. Risk & Performance Ratios
- **Alpha ($\alpha$)**: Measures the performance of a stock or fund relative to its benchmark index (e.g., Nifty 50).
  - *Interpretation*: $\alpha = 1$ means the stock outperformed the benchmark by 1%, and $\alpha = -1$ means it underperformed by 1%.
- **Beta ($\beta$)**: Measures the volatility or systemic risk of a security or portfolio in comparison to the market as a whole.
  - *Interpretation*: $\beta > 1$ means the stock is more volatile than the market, and $\beta < 1$ means it is less volatile.
- **Sharpe Ratio**: Measures the risk-adjusted return of an investment, calculating the excess return per unit of total deviation.
  - *Interpretation*: A Sharpe ratio between 1 and 3 (or above) is considered good to excellent, representing higher returns relative to the risk taken.
- **Sortino Ratio**: A variation of the Sharpe ratio that only penalizes negative volatility (downside risk), rather than total volatility.
  - *Interpretation*: The higher the Sortino ratio, the better the investment's return for its bad risk. A Sortino ratio greater than 2 is considered good.

## 4. Important Corporate Actions

Corporate actions are decisions made by a company’s board of directors that directly affect its stakeholders and its stock structure:

### A. Dividends
A portion of a company’s earnings distributed to its shareholders, usually paid out in cash per share.
- **Purpose**: Rewarding shareholders and demonstrating company financial health.
- **Effect on Stock**: On the "ex-dividend date", the share price typically drops by the dividend amount.

### B. Stock Splits
An action where a company divides its existing shares into multiple shares to boost liquidity (e.g., a 1:5 split turns 1 share into 5).
- **Purpose**: Lowering the share price to make it more affordable for retail investors.
- **Effect on Stock**: Total shares increase, share price decreases proportionally, but the overall market capitalization remains unchanged.

### C. Bonus Shares
Free additional shares given to existing shareholders based on their current holding ratio (e.g., a 1:1 bonus gives 1 free share for every 1 share owned).
- **Purpose**: Utilizing accumulated reserves to reward investors without cash outflows.
- **Effect on Stock**: Similar to a stock split; shares increase, price drops proportionally, market cap remains unchanged.

### D. Buybacks (Share Repurchases)
A transaction where a company repurchases its own shares from the open market or directly from shareholders.
- **Purpose**: Reducing the supply of shares to increase Earnings Per Share (EPS), support stock value, or return excess cash.
- **Effect on Stock**: Decreases outstanding shares, usually pushing the stock price upward.
