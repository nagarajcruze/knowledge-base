# Mutual Funds

My personal learning notes on mutual fund categories, investment methods, and fund selection strategies.

---

## 1. Categories of Mutual Funds

Mutual funds are pooled investment vehicles categorized by their asset classes, structures, and investment strategies:

### A. By Asset Class
1. **Equity Funds**: Invest primarily in equity shares of companies. They offer high returns but carry high market risk.
   - **Large-Cap**: Invest in the top 100 companies by market capitalization (stable, lower risk).
   - **Mid-Cap**: Invest in companies ranked 101–250 (higher growth potential, moderate-to-high risk).
   - **Small-Cap**: Invest in companies ranked 251 and below (very high growth potential, extremely volatile).
   - **ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)**: Tax-saving equity mutual funds with a statutory 3-year lock-in period.
   - **Sectoral/Thematic**: Invest in specific sectors (e.g., IT, Banking, Pharma) or themes (e.g., Infrastructure, ESG). High concentration risk.
2. **Debt Funds**: Invest in fixed-income securities like Government Bonds (G-Secs), Corporate Debentures, Treasury Bills, and Commercial Paper.
   - **Liquid/Overnight Funds**: Invest in extremely short-term debt instruments (maturing in 1 to 91 days). Used to park emergency cash safely with high liquidity.
   - **Gilt Funds**: Invest only in government securities (no credit risk, but subject to interest rate fluctuations).
3. **Hybrid/Balanced Funds**: Invest in a mix of both equity and debt instruments to achieve a balance between wealth generation and capital preservation.

### B. By Management Style
1. **Active Funds**: Managed actively by a fund manager who researches, selects, and trades stocks/bonds to outperform a target benchmark index. Carries higher expense ratios.
2. **Passive Funds (Index Funds & ETFs)**: Replicate a specific market index (e.g., Nifty 50, S&P 500) by holding the exact same assets in the same proportions. They aim to match, not beat, index performance. They have significantly lower expense ratios.

### C. By Structure
1. **Open-Ended Funds**: Allow investors to enter (buy) or exit (sell) units at any time based on the daily Net Asset Value (NAV). Highly liquid.
2. **Close-Ended Funds**: Have a fixed maturity period (e.g., 3 or 5 years). Units can only be purchased during the New Fund Offer (NFO) and redeemed at maturity. (Units are listed on stock exchanges for liquidity, but trading volumes are typically low).

## 2. SIP vs. Lumpsum & Rupee Cost Averaging

Investors can invest in mutual funds and stocks in two ways:

### A. Investment Methods
- **SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)**: Investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals (e.g., monthly).
  - **Best For**: Salaried individuals, building regular investing habits, and volatile markets.
- **Lumpsum**: Investing a large, one-time amount.
  - **Best For**: Windfalls, bonuses, or when markets undergo a major correction.

### B. Rupee Cost Averaging
This is the primary advantage of a SIP. Since you invest a fixed amount of money regularly:
- When the market is **high**, your fixed amount buys **fewer units**.
- When the market is **low** (in a correction), your fixed amount buys **more units**.
- Over time, this averages out the acquisition cost per unit, eliminating the need to time the market.

## 3. Filtering Index Funds

When choosing an index fund, evaluate the following parameters:
1. **AUM (Assets Under Management)**: Higher AUM represents stability, liquidity, and trust.
2. **Expense Ratio**: The annual fee charged by the fund. Lower is always better for long-term compound growth.
3. **Tracking Error**: The difference in performance between the index fund and its benchmark index. Lower tracking error indicates the fund is replicating the index more accurately.

## 4. Index & Fund Comparisons

- **Nifty Next 50 (NN50) vs. Midcap 150 vs. Smallcap 250**: Compare asset classes for risk/reward.
- **Why Midcap 150 is often preferred over Nifty Next 50**:
  - **Diversification**: NN50 is concentrated in only 50 stocks, whereas Midcap 150 is spread across 150 stocks.
  - **Liquidity & Impact Cost**: Both indices can experience high impact costs, but Midcap 150's broader diversification makes it slightly safer and more stable than NN50.
- **Nifty 500 Value Index Fund**: Often preferred over momentum strategies for value-focused, long-term investments.
