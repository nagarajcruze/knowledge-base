## 1. Personal Financial Planning Essentials

Before investing in high-risk assets like equity or mutual funds, you should secure your financial baseline:

### A. Emergency Fund
- **Purpose**: A safety net to cover unexpected expenses (e.g., job loss, medical emergencies).
- **Rule of Thumb**: Save **6 to 12 months** of your fixed monthly expenses.
- **Where to park**: Keep this money in highly liquid instruments like savings accounts, high-yield fixed deposits (FDs), or liquid mutual funds.

### B. Health Insurance (Mediclaim)
- **Purpose**: Protects your accumulated wealth from being wiped out by high hospitalization bills.
- **Key Checkpoints**: Look for plans with no room-rent capping, restore benefits, and minimal co-payments. Keep a policy separate from your corporate/employer-provided policy, as corporate policies end if you leave your job.

### C. Term Insurance (Life Insurance)
- **Purpose**: Provides financial security to your dependents/family in the event of your untimely demise.
- **Key Principle**: Buy a **pure term insurance policy** (which pays out only on death) rather than investment-linked policies (like ULIPs or endowment plans, which charge high fees and offer sub-par returns).
- **Sum Assured Rule**: Aim for a cover of at least **10x to 15x of your annual income**.

## 2. The Power of Compounding & Inflation

Understanding these two opposing forces is fundamental to long-term wealth building:

### A. The Power of Compounding
Compounding is the process where an investment earns interest or returns, and those earnings are reinvested to earn additional returns over time (earning interest on interest).

- **The Formula**:
  $$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$
  *(Where $A$ is the final amount, $P$ is the principal, $r$ is the annual interest rate, $t$ is the time in years, and $n$ is the compounding frequency).*
- **Rule of 72**: A simple shortcut to estimate how long it will take for your money to double at a given rate of return:
  $$\text{Years to Double} \approx \frac{72}{\text{Annual Rate of Return}}$$
  *Example: At an average equity return of 12%, your money will double in approximately $72 / 12 = 6$ years.*

### B. The Impact of Inflation
Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, eroding the purchasing power of money over time.

- **Nominal vs. Real Returns**:
  - **Nominal Return**: The percentage increase in your money before accounting for inflation.
  - **Real Return**: The actual growth of your purchasing power after subtracting inflation.
  $$\text{Real Return} \approx \text{Nominal Return} - \text{Inflation}$$
  *Example: If a fixed deposit pays a nominal 6% interest, but inflation is running at 5%, your real growth in purchasing power is only 1%.*
- **Why Equities Matter**: Traditional low-risk savings (like savings accounts or fixed deposits) often struggle to beat inflation after taxes. Equities, despite short-term volatility, have historically delivered real, inflation-beating returns over the long term.
