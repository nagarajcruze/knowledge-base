# Display Panel Technologies

This guide compares the primary display technologies used in modern TVs, computer monitors, and smartphones.

---

## 1. LCD (Liquid Crystal Display)
LCD is the most common display technology. Liquid crystals do not emit light directly; instead, they use a **backlight** (typically LEDs) to illuminate the screen.

### How it Works
1.  **Backlight:** A light source shines white light from behind the display.
2.  **Polarizing Filters:** Filter the light waves into a single direction.
3.  **Liquid Crystals:** Electric currents stimulate these crystals, causing them to twist and untwist. This controls how much light passes through the red, green, and blue subpixel filters.
4.  **Color Filters:** The light passes through RGB subpixels to create the final colored image.

### LCD Panel Variants: IPS vs. VA vs. TN
LCD panels are categorized based on how the liquid crystals are aligned and behave:

| Panel Type | Viewing Angles | Contrast Ratio | Color Accuracy | Response Time | Best Suited For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TN** (Twisted Nematic) | Poor | Low (~1000:1) | Average | **Fastest** (<1ms) | Competitive gaming, budget screens |
| **IPS** (In-Plane Switching) | **Excellent** | Medium (~1000:1) | **Excellent** | Fast (1-4ms) | Color-critical work (design, editing), office |
| **VA** (Vertical Alignment) | Moderate | **High** (3000:1 - 5000:1) | Very Good | Medium (4-10ms) | Watching movies/media in dark rooms |

## 2. OLED (Organic Light Emitting Diode)
Unlike LCDs, **OLED** displays do not use a backlight. Every single pixel in an OLED panel is **self-emissive**—meaning it generates its own light and color when an electric current is applied.

### Advantages of OLED
*   **Infinite Contrast:** When a pixel needs to display black, it turns off completely, emitting zero light. This achieves a perfect contrast ratio (True Black).
*   **Response Time:** Near-instantaneous pixel response time (typically <0.1ms), eliminating motion blur and ghosting.
*   **Viewing Angles:** Colors and brightness remain consistent even at extreme angles.
*   **Thinner Design:** No backlight layer allows OLED screens to be incredibly thin and flexible.

### Limitations of OLED
*   **Burn-in Risk:** Static images displayed for prolonged periods can leave a permanent ghost image (burn-in).
*   **Peak Brightness:** Generally lower peak brightness than premium LCD displays, making them less ideal for highly sunlit rooms.

---

## 3. QLED (Quantum Dot LED)
**QLED** is a premium variation of LCD technology popularized by Samsung. It is NOT self-emissive like OLED. Instead, it is a standard LCD screen with a layer of **Quantum Dots** placed between the backlight and the liquid crystals.

### How Quantum Dots Work
Quantum dots are tiny semiconductor particles (nanocrystals) that emit highly precise wavelengths of light when hit by the blue backlight. This allows the display to achieve:
*   **Extreme Brightness:** QLED screens can achieve very high brightness levels (up to 2,000+ nits), making them excellent for HDR.
*   **Vibrant Color Volume:** Offers a much wider range of colors that stay saturated even at peak brightness.

### Contrast in QLED
To compete with OLED's black levels, high-end QLED displays use **FALD (Full Array Local Dimming)** or **Mini-LED** backlights. This divides the backlight into hundreds or thousands of individual zones that can dim or turn off independently, although they still suffer from minor "blooming" (light halos around bright objects on dark backgrounds).
